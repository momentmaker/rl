🎲 *Random Learning — 2026-09-23*

Three subjects where the authoritative thing stopped being authoritative — a theory, a reference list, a translator's name.

*1. Campbell's monomyth, and what folklorists actually said*
The academic objection was never "overused." It was that the pattern was built by discarding the counter-examples. Barre Toelken: Campbell "could construct a monomyth of the hero only by citing those stories that fit his preconceived mold, and leaving out equally valid stories… which did not fit the pattern."

Alan Dundes was blunter — Campbell was a "non-expert," and "there is no single idea promulgated by amateurs that has done more harm to serious folklore study than the notion of archetype."

• *It wasn't even first.* Otto Rank published a hero-myth pattern in *1909*, Lord Raglan in *1936*.

• *The version that won isn't Campbell's.* Christopher Vogler condensed him into a *seven-page studio memo* — the twelve-stage list every blog reproduces. Rank → Campbell's 17 stages → Vogler's 12 → a template.

• *The best reply this month supplies a shape instead of attacking one.* The Irish *immram*, a voyage where return fails: in *immram Bran* the voyager refuses to land, and the companion who touches earth crumbles to ash. Departure-initiation-return can't express that. The essay's line: "a failure of supply, not of imagination."

• *It's now a dropdown.* One app markets "nine story structures ready… switch freely," putting a claim about all human storytelling third in a list, next to Kishotenketsu — a form with no conflict in it.

*2. Printer tracking dots, and why you can't check yours*
That colour lasers stamp every page with a hidden yellow code — device, date, time — is settled across thirteen manufacturers. What's gone is any way to verify it for *your* machine.

• *EFF's printer list is retired:* "This list is no longer being updated." The caveat matters more — a "no" proves nothing, because manufacturers may use methods that aren't visible dots.

• *The decoder is frozen.* DEDA, the TU Dresden toolkit, 2.6K stars, *last pushed September 2024*. Its open issues are the failure mode: "unrecognised dots," "Cant detect a pattern." The README concedes it "may not recognise newer or divergent tracking patterns."

• *Anonymisation works by addition* — print *more* yellow dots on top to overflow the pattern.

• *All surviving advice is one sentence:* buy a black-and-white toner printer. Monochrome carries no dots.

The inversion worth holding: with AI-generated media the marking method is *published*, so the fight is about removal. Here it was never published, was reverse-engineered twice, and both efforts stopped. The fight isn't removal — it's that you can't look.

*3. Tao Te Ching translations*
"Which translation" is really two questions, and the second is invisible.

The received text is Wang Bi's (d. 249 CE). The *Mawangdui* manuscripts, from a tomb sealed in 168 BCE, **reverse the book's order** — *Te* before *Tao*. The *Guodian* slips, pre-300 BCE, hold about *40%* of it.

• *The most-quoted version is furthest from the Chinese, and says so.* Stephen Mitchell's is subtitled "A New English Version." A million-plus copies. He doesn't read Chinese; it re-renders prior English translations.

• *Engagement inverts usefulness.* A context-free Mitchell quote: *77 likes*. The month's one reasoned recommendation — "get an annotated edition… D.C. Lau (Penguin) or Red Pine's with classical commentaries" — *1 like*.

• *Attribution degrades in transit.* One post credits a Mitchell passage to a "Stephen Miller translation." An r/taoism thread is someone holding a book, unable to tell which of 250+ versions it is.

• *Someone built the fix:* a site collating all 81 chapters against the Wang Bi text by machine. Bluntest finding — "many English 'Lao Tzu quotes' in circulation have no source." Also: *wuwei* isn't inaction, it's "withholding the unnecessary."

Why so many versions? Grammar: classical Chinese marks no tense, number, case, person, voice or mood.
