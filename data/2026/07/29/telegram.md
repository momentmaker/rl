🎲 *Random Learning — 2026-07-29*

Three rabbit holes, one shape: the label on the box is not the thing in the box.

*1. Does a second AI reviewer find real bugs, or just noise?*
The number that settles it: someone ran CodeRabbit, Sentry Seer, Greptile and Cursor BugBot *simultaneously* over 146 real PRs for three and a half weeks, logging 617 distinct flagged locations. *93.4% were caught by exactly one of the four tools.* 6% by two, none by all four. They never once flagged the same line. So a second reviewer isn't a confidence vote on the first, it's a nearly disjoint net — whichever one you picked is showing you a minority of what's there. Why a second *model* and not a second pass: LLM evaluators favour their own generations, and the better a model is at recognising its own output, the more generously it scores it (Panickssery, NeurIPS 2024). That's the case for the cross-provider loop: not that Codex is better, but that its biases don't overlap. The bill is noise — Greptile catches 82% of bugs vs CodeRabbit's 44%, at ~11 false positives per run vs 2, and CR-Bench found low signal-to-noise is what drives abandonment. A noisy reviewer gets switched off, not tuned. And the demand is downstream of something dumber: AI made PRs *3.5x bigger* and defect detection went *down*. Best line: "The real trap isn't the volume, it's the aesthetics. AI code compiles and looks structurally sound, so it bypasses the skepticism you'd normally have."

*2. Where 4-bit quantization stops being good enough*
The premise breaks before you reach an answer, because "Q4_K_M" is a family name, not a number. A tool called picchio measured four quantizations of the *same* Qwen3.5-9B, all wearing the identical Q4_K_M label: *5.02, 5.02, 5.07 and 5.27 bits per weight.* K-quants allocate mixed precision across sub-blocks, so two files with the same name are not the same file. The cliff is task-dependent and sits a step earlier than chat: *Q3 and below degrade tool-call reliability before they degrade chat quality*, and reasoning degrades faster than perplexity. Q3 breaks multi-step pipelines that worked at Q4 — reads as "the model got dumber," is really the model losing the ability to emit a well-formed call. The uncomfortable pair of numbers: 4-bit costs *1-3% on tool use* but *10-15% on real-world application accuracy.* The easy metric barely moves; the one you care about moves a lot. Floor is unchanged (Q4_K_M, ~96.5% of baseline) plus one rule: *a smaller quant that stays on the GPU beats a bigger one that silently spills to CPU.* The 30-day movement is that sub-4-bit is becoming a *training* problem — PTQ is fine at 4-bit and up, QAT wins below it, which is why Google, Apple and Meta now ship quantization-aware checkpoints themselves.

*3. Why server-sent events came back*
SSE won LLM token streaming by default — Anthropic, OpenAI and Google all stream over it — for unglamorous reasons. Token streaming is one-way, so full duplex is capability you pay for and never use. SSE is plain HTTP, so every proxy, CDN and firewall already handles it, and statelessness means no sticky sessions. The one durable objection died quietly: the six-connections-per-origin cap was an HTTP/1.1 artifact, and on HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 streams multiplex, so "WebSockets scale better" no longer holds. The surprise is that *MCP went the other way* — HTTP+SSE deprecated for Streamable HTTP because the two-connection design has no native resumability and is hostile to load balancers, serverless and firewalls. That resolves cleanly: what got deprecated is the two-endpoint *pattern*, not the `text/event-stream` format, which Streamable HTTP still returns. The tax is all in middleboxes with hostile defaults — nginx buffers proxied responses by default, so the client sees nothing for minutes, and native EventSource is GET-only with no custom headers. Best cautionary tale: a dev whose *ISP blocks persistent streaming connections* — every deploy timed out awaiting confirmation even when the transaction had landed on-chain.
