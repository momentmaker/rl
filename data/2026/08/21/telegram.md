🎲 *Random Learning — 2026-08-21*

Three cases where the thing you paid for turns out to be a rental.

*1. Code signing and notarization for indie desktop apps*
CA/Browser Forum Ballot CSC-31 caps code-signing certificate validity at *460 days*, down from a 39-month ceiling, effective *1 March 2026*. The reason is the thesis of the whole topic: revocation isn't trusted to contain a leaked signing key, so they bounded how long one can keep signing. Meanwhile the thing indies paid the EV premium for was *withdrawn two years ago*: EV stopped bypassing SmartScreen in 2024 and all certs now build reputation identically. You can't buy past the waiting, so the warning falls hardest on developers with the least distribution. One person measured the cost — roughly *6 of 10 Mac downloads reach first launch, against 2 of 15 on Windows*. It drew *4 points and 1 comment* on HN. Microsoft's managed service is the cheap escape at *$9.99/month* — Windows-only, gated to verified businesses in four jurisdictions. The failure nobody plans for: transgui merged a fix for signatures carrying *no trusted timestamp*, so past releases silently go unsigned when the cert expires — now on a 15-month clock. Same 30 days: an open-source project *crowdfunding a certificate* while malware sideloads payloads through *valid Authenticode signatures*.

*2. Preference post-training for small models*
The method question is closed: SFT, then DPO if you have a preference labeler, GRPO if you have a verifier. So the arguments moved to the *data*. DPO's implicit reward tracks sequence length, and the alarming number is how little contamination it takes — *fewer than 1% of pairs preferring the longer output* bakes strong length bias into a reward model, compounding each self-alignment round. The canonical receipt is clinical: CheXalign report length went *63 words to 158* after DPO with *no factual gain*. A model measurably more preferred and no better. So the artifact of the month is a *diagnostic, not a method*: check whether surface lexical patterns alone separate chosen from rejected, before training. At small scale the recipe stops transferring — identical GRPO on 353M/316M/672M from-scratch models gave *three different outcomes, no clean relationship to scale* — and the binding constraint is the reward function, not the algorithm. Below some capability level the model finds the reward's holes faster than the task. Meanwhile the VRAM floor collapsed: layer streaming trains *8B at 119.6 tok/s in 3.32 GB* on a 4 GB laptop GPU. Attention sits on weights arriving, not on what you do to them: the best methods post took 29 upvotes while Qwen threads took thousands.

*3. Devices bricked when the servers go dark*
The clarifying event isn't a shutdown. On *18 August* a server-side failure took Nest Hubs, Minis and Home speakers offline for nearly *12 hours* worldwide, with no status channel during and no root cause after. Google is solvent and committed to the line; the hardware was inert until someone fixed a server. Bankruptcy is just the dramatic version of a dependency that fails routinely. Bose ran the graceful version — SoundTouch cloud ended 6 May, but a final update preserved Bluetooth, AirPlay and grouping, and earned real praise, which tells you how low the bar sits. Belkin ran the other version: most Wemo devices lost support *31 Jan 2026*, and owners are now in arbitration. The loudest thing in the window isn't anger — *40,333 upvotes* for someone reporting a 15-year-old GPS still getting free map updates, top comment "Tom tom stopped mine years ago." The response isn't lobbying, it's *reimplementation*: OpenCloudTouch for SoundTouch, a GPLv3 binary to archive the Blink clips the subscription throws away, ESP32 builds replacing bought thermostats. Local-first is a spectrum — Home Assistant Green still took an hour to reconnect to its own cloud. Repair law doesn't cover this: its nearest clause only requires repair *tools* to work offline, and R2R was quietly stripped from the 2026 NDAA.
