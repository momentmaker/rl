🎲 *Random Learning — 2026-08-02*

Three fields where the practitioners aren't posting. Every number below came from a config file, a preprint or a vendor blog — so read the denominator.

*1. What coding agents actually run inside: sandbox runtimes, prices and escapes*
Venture money everywhere, public argument nowhere: twelve Reddit threads across seven relevant subs name *zero* sandbox providers; the whole HN layer is *132 points across 19 stories*. The month's movement is security, and the mechanism inverts the name — eight disclosures in four days across four agent tools, and *most never breached the sandbox*. They exploit files the agent itself writes, which trusted host software then processes outside the boundary (Cursor's is now CVE-2026-48124). Every cold-start number traces to a vendor blog; the honest decomposition is *~49ms snapshot-restore inside ~179ms p50, ~203ms p99*. Prices have converged to near-parity — *Daytona and E2B both $0.0504/vCPU-hr*, Modal ~$0.071, Cloudflare $0.072 of *active* CPU — so the meter is the decision, not the rate: under a wall-clock meter you pay per second "for a virtual machine to sit perfectly still and watch a language model think." One source alone names the constraint likeliest to force a rewrite: a *30-minute* execution cap on Cloudflare against *24 hours* on E2B. And both sides of the isolation argument land in one place — escape hardening is over-invested in, *egress policy* is what matters.

*2. What happens when AI-designed proteins reach the bench*
BoltzGen's headline *66%* is per *target*, not per design: at least one nanomolar binder for 66% of novel targets after testing *15 or fewer* designs each — conservatively, 6 of 9 genuinely novel ones. The most useful document is a published *negative* result: RFdiffusion detection binders, 6 targets × 5 designs, *all 30 tested*, and only the Strep-Tag II peptide produced anything — every eukaryotic target lost to low expression, nonspecific binding or undetectable affinity, so the bottleneck sits outside the model. Across the largest pooled experimental set (*3,766 characterised binders, 15 targets*) success runs *10% on HER2 to 60% on SpCas9*: success is a property of the target, not the tool — so cross-paper comparisons don't hold. ProtDBench makes that mechanical — substantial *verifier-dependent bias*, meaning the filter you evaluate with picks the winner. Meanwhile generation outruns validation by two orders of magnitude: one July preprint emitted *306,146* candidates covering 40.9% of the human proteome, *zero* wet-lab tested. And per PNAS on 24 July, predictors routinely bury ionizable residues in hydrophobic cores while still reporting the right fold — confidence is geometric plausibility, not correctness.

*3. How speech-native models actually represent audio*
The semantic-versus-acoustic distinction is *one integer in a config file*: Mimi ships `num_quantizers=32` with `num_semantic_quantizers=1`, that single codebook filled by distilling WavLM into RVQ level one — 12.5 Hz, *1.1 kbps*, 80 ms frame, so 80 ms of streaming latency. (The docs contradict each other on 12 vs 12.5 Hz and 16 vs 32 codebooks — read the config, not the prose.) EnCodec states the knob plainly: bitrate *is* RVQ depth, n_q=2 for 1.5 kbps through n_q=32 for 24 kbps on one encoder. What breaks at low bitrate isn't fidelity, it's the *seam between frames* — audible popping between decoder frames, fixed with a sliding-window detokenizer, not more bits. And the one controlled test of the low-frame-rate cliff calls it a *training artifact*: 12.5 → 6.25 Hz sends WER from 10.62% to *107.40%* under fixed clip duration, but only to 15.37% when token-sequence length is held constant. The tell: every canonical codec repo is frozen upstream (EnCodec last pushed Jan 2024, SNAC Nov 2024, DAC's main branch *2023*), all in-window commits land in inference runtimes, and the month's two biggest speech stories — sub-500kb ASR+TTS at 570 points, Kokoro at 515 — use no codec tokens at all.
